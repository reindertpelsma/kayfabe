--- 3. THE PER-LAUNCH ARITHMETIC — the only place the two halves meet
    ⊘⊘ THE HOST CENSUS IS WHOLE-BOOT. It includes every doorbell rung during
       driver load, cuInit, cuCtxCreate, the copies and the batch phase — not
       only the timed launches. ⇒ dividing it by the launch count is an
       OVER-ESTIMATE of the per-launch trap cost, and that is the useful
       direction: if even the over-estimate is small against launch_ms, the
       floor is NOT inside our traps, and no clock correspondence was needed
       to say so. ⚠ The converse does NOT follow — a large over-estimate does
       not locate the time in the launches.
    launches in the run (incl. first)         = 12
    doorbell MMIO writes                      = 400
    ⇒ doorbell writes per launch (OVER-EST)   = 33.33
    ⇒ host doorbell-trap ms per launch (O-EST)= 1220.861 ms
    ⇒ host time IN OTHER MMIO TRAPS per launch=  404.534 ms
    guest median launch_ms                    =  108.931 ms
    ⇒ ACCOUNTED (all MMIO traps)              = 1625.395 ms  (1492.1%)
    ⇒ ★★★ UNACCOUNTED                        = -1516.464 ms  (-1392.1%)
      ⊘ UNACCOUNTED is time the guest spent NOT inside one of our traps: its own
        driver, its own userspace spin, or waiting for something we do not run.
        It is NAMED here and must not be attributed to any host segment.
      ⚠⚠ ACCOUNTED EXCEEDS the guest's launch window. Nesting says that is
         IMPOSSIBLE ⇒ the doorbell population is not per-launch (start-up
         traffic is in the total), or the clocks are not what this assumes.
         ⊘ REPORT THIS AS OUTCOME (D)/(E) — do not rescale to make it fit.

--- 3b. ⊘⊘ THE BENCH IS NESTED — what UNACCOUNTED could be, and what it cannot
    `[measured 2026-08-14]` the bench host is ITSELF a KVM guest, so our guest runs
    at L2 and every MMIO access is a NESTED vmexit (L2 -> L1 -> L0). The C artifact
    attributes a 2.5x llama.cpp gap to exactly this (49.9 tok/s bare metal vs ~20
    nested) — `C: docs/MILESTONES.md:12-14`.
    ⊘ NO SEGMENT ABOVE CAN CONTAIN IT. The exit is already over when our handler is
      entered, so nested-virt tax lives ENTIRELY in UNACCOUNTED. What bounds it is
      the exit COUNT x the per-exit cost.
    our device's MMIO accesses this boot (each ONE vmexit) = 429200
      reads=12000  doorbell_writes=400  other_writes=416800
    ⇒ per launch (WHOLE-BOOT OVER-ESTIMATE)                = 35767
      at   2 us per nested exit ⇒   71.533 ms per launch
      at  10 us per nested exit ⇒  357.667 ms per launch
      at  50 us per nested exit ⇒ 1788.333 ms per launch
      ⚠ THE PER-EXIT COST IS NOT MEASURED HERE. Those three rows are an
        ARITHMETIC RANGE, not a finding — exactly the kind of reasoning that
        produced `251/2 = 125.5` and was wrong. They bound the question; they
        do not answer it.
    ⚠ And our device is not the only source of exits: the KVM sampler log
      (`run_<tag>_kvmexits.log`) counts ALL of them, host-wide.
